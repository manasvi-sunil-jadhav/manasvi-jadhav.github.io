Two Speeds of Recovery: Comparing Domestic and Foreign Tourism's Response to COVID-19 in India (2011–2022)

DATA ANALYSIS · PYTHON

This project examines how the COVID-19 pandemic affected domestic and foreign tourism in India differently, and compares the speed and strength of their recovery after the shock.

Project Overview

The COVID-19 pandemic disrupted India's tourism sector, but its impact was not uniform across visitor types. Domestic and foreign tourist visits declined and recovered at markedly different rates.

While domestic tourism showed greater resilience and a quicker rebound, foreign tourist visits experienced a deeper decline and took longer to recover. This project uses official Ministry of Tourism data to quantify and compare the differential impact of COVID-19 on domestic and foreign tourism in India.

Research Question

How did the COVID-19 pandemic differentially impact domestic and foreign tourism in India, and which segment has shown a faster and stronger recovery?

Project Objectives

  • Compare trends in Domestic Tourist Visits (DTVs) and Foreign Tourist Visits (FTVs) in India from 2011 onwards, and examine how COVID-19 affected both segments.
  • Measure post-COVID recovery by calculating each year's visits as a percentage of the 2019 level, and identify when domestic and foreign tourism reached 50%, 90%, and 100% of their pre-COVID levels.
  • Forecast the future trend of Foreign Tourist Visits (FTVs) using data from 2011 onwards and estimate when, or if, foreign tourism may return to its 2019 level.

Dataset & Source

The project uses official tourism statistics published by the Ministry of Tourism, Government of India. The source dataset contains year-wise Domestic Tourist Visits (DTVs) and Foreign Tourist Visits (FTVs) for India for 1991, 2001, and 2011–2022.

For this analysis, the study period is restricted to 2011–2022 in order to examine the pre-COVID, COVID-shock, and recovery periods.

Ministry of Tourism Government of India 2011–2022 Domestic Tourism Foreign Tourism

Dataset: Year-wise Details of Domestic and Foreign Tourist Visits

Variables

Dependent Variable Annual Tourist Visits, measured separately for Domestic and Foreign tourism.
Time Year of observation, covering the 2011–2022 analysis period.
Tourist Category Domestic Tourist Visits (DTVs) and Foreign Tourist Visits (FTVs).
Period Phase Pre-COVID, COVID-shock, and recovery phases, represented as categorical variables.

Python Methodology

Python is used throughout the project for data cleaning, transformation, statistical analysis, visualisation, and predictive modelling.

pandas NumPy Matplotlib Seaborn Linear Regression Decision Tree Regression
  • Data Cleaning & Structuring: pandas is used to organise the tourism data and prepare it for analysis.
  • Numerical Analysis: NumPy is used for calculations such as growth rates and recovery measures.
  • Descriptive Analysis: Summary statistics such as mean and standard deviation are used to understand the tourism trends.
  • Visualisation: Matplotlib and Seaborn are used to compare domestic and foreign tourism trends and illustrate the COVID-19 shock and subsequent recovery.

Modelling Plan

The project will model Domestic Tourist Visits and Foreign Tourist Visits separately in order to compare their response to the COVID-19 shock and subsequent recovery.

Target Variable Tourist Visits – Domestic (Model A) and Tourist Visits – Foreign (Model B)
Predictor Variables Year and Period Phase (Pre-COVID / COVID-shock / Recovery)
Baseline Model Linear Regression
Comparison Model Decision Tree Regression
Evaluation Metrics MAE, RMSE, and MAPE

Reason for Model Selection

Linear Regression serves as an interpretable baseline that captures the overall growth trend against Year. Decision Tree Regression is used for comparison because it can capture the sharp, non-linear structural break caused by COVID-19 that a straight-line model cannot represent, using the Period Phase feature to separate behaviour before, during, and after the shock.

Expected Outcome

The Foreign tourism model is expected to show a larger COVID-era error and a slower projected return to its 2019 baseline compared to the Domestic tourism model. This expectation is based on the recovery ratios observed during the preliminary analysis, where Foreign Tourist Visits had recovered to a lower percentage of their 2019 level than Domestic Tourist Visits by 2022.
